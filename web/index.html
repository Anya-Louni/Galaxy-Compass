Galaxies Without a Compass

    Galaxies Without a Compass

    Learning Under Rotational Symmetry

    Anya Louni
    Scroll

    Abstract

    Galaxy morphology carries no preferred orientation. A spiral photographed at any position angle belongs to the same class, and the classifier that reads it should return the same answer. This work encodes that symmetry in the architecture of a convolutional network using E(2)-steerable convolutions, then measures what the encoding is worth against a dense baseline matched in convolutional cost and trained with the same full-circle rotation and reflection augmentation.

    On Galaxy10 DECaLS the steerable network reaches the baseline's fully supervised accuracy using 14 percent of the labels and exceeds it by 4.7 points at full supervision, while carrying 11.8 times fewer parameters at identical convolutional throughput. Its predictions are invariant to the four grid rotations to numerical precision. Averaging the baseline over the dihedral orbit at inference time recovers 0.36 points for eight times the compute, leaving the separation intact. The same encoder trained without labels under a contrastive objective supports a k-nearest-neighbour probe at 67.2 percent and yields a two-dimensional atlas of all 17,736 galaxies, from which a combined reconstruction and isolation score ranks candidate anomalies.

    1

    Orientation as nuisance

    Every morphology label in Galaxy Zoo describes shape. Position angle on the sky is set by the geometry of the observation, and it varies with the telescope's pointing and the survey's scan pattern.

    A classifier trained on such images faces a choice of representation. It can learn a separate response for each orientation of each structure, spending capacity and labels on a degree of freedom that carries no information about the class. It can be shown rotated copies during training and asked to infer that the rotations are equivalent. Or the equivalence can be written into the operator itself, so the response transforms with the input by construction.

    The third option is the subject of this work. E(2)-steerable convolutions constrain the filter bank to a basis that commutes with the action of a chosen symmetry group, so a rotated input produces a correspondingly rotated feature map at every layer. Reducing over the group axis at the end yields a representation that is invariant before any weight is trained.

    The question is what that constraint buys once a competent baseline has been given the same symmetry through augmentation. The experiments below hold convolutional cost, augmentation, optimisation budget and evaluation protocol fixed, vary only the operator, and measure the difference in how many labels each architecture requires.

    2

    Data

    Galaxy10 DECaLS provides 17,736 colour cutouts assembled from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys in the g, r and z bands, labelled into ten morphology classes from aggregated Galaxy Zoo votes. Preprocessing verifies the source file by SHA-256 and asserts the ten published per-class counts before writing any derived array, so a truncated or substituted file halts the pipeline.

    Images are cropped from 256 to 224 pixels, stored at 95 pixels, and presented to the network at 65. The stored margin exists to keep rotation augmentation well posed: a 95 pixel frame rotated through any angle still covers the central 65 pixel field with observed sky, since 95 divided by the square root of two is 67.2. Cropping tighter would admit undefined corners whose orientation the network can read in place of the morphology.

    The crop is set from the data. Stacking the radial surface brightness profile of all 17,736 galaxies places 96.9 percent of the light above sky inside the crop, with the profile at the crop edge sitting at 1.4 percent of its central value. Splits are stratified under a fixed seed at 12,415 training, 1,772 validation and 3,549 test galaxies.

    Colour is a measurement

    Rotation, reflection and small translations are applied freely, since each is an exact symmetry of the labelling function. The three channels are photometric bands, and their ratios encode stellar population age, dust content and redshift. Brightness, contrast and hue jitter are standard on natural images and would destroy that signal, so the augmentation pipeline leaves colour untouched and perturbs only with additive noise, which models the detector and the sky background.

    3

    Architecture and controls

    Both networks share a width schedule of 32, 64, 128 and 256 channels, kernels of 5, 3, 3 and 3, and four stages of antialiased blur-pool downsampling. The steerable model realises each width as copies of the regular representation of the cyclic group C8; the dense model uses plain channels. Because e2cnn expands its steerable basis into a dense filter bank and calls the same convolution, the two perform an identical 75.4 million multiply-accumulates per image.

    Weight sharing across the group is the mechanism that produces equivariance, so the steerable model spends fewer weights per layer. The dense baseline therefore holds 393,514 parameters against 33,286, a factor of 11.8. The baseline receives the larger parameter budget, the same compute, the same full-circle rotation and reflection augmentation, and the same step budget. Runs share a fixed number of gradient steps rather than a fixed number of epochs, so a model trained on all the labels receives the same optimisation budget as one trained on a twentieth of them.

    Why the input is 65 pixels wide

    A steerable convolution is equivariant to the precision of the arithmetic. Stride-2 subsampling introduces a second condition. On an even grid the centre of rotation falls between pixels, the retained lattice maps to a shifted lattice under a quarter turn, and the discrepancy compounds through the depth of the network. An odd grid fixes the centre pixel and preserves the lattice.

    The effect is large enough to determine the design. Measured drift of the representation under a quarter turn falls from 4.7×10-2 at 64 pixels to 8.8×10-8 at 65. A single steerable layer tests at 5.5×10-7 relative error on the exact grid rotations.

    Averaging over the orbit

    An invariant prediction is available without a steerable architecture. Evaluating an ordinary network on every rotation of the input and averaging the probabilities produces an exactly invariant output, for the same reason group pooling does, at a cost of one forward pass per group element.

    Every checkpoint was therefore re-scored with predictions averaged over the eight element dihedral orbit, four quarter turns and their reflections, each a lossless permutation of pixels. Both architectures received the identical treatment.

    7

    Structure without labels

    The same steerable encoder was trained under a contrastive objective on the training split alone, with no labels at any point. The transformations it is asked to discount are those a telescope could have produced differently: orientation, parity, pointing, seeing-scale and noise realisation. Rotation invariance arrives from the architecture, so the objective spends its capacity on the remainder.

    Ranking the unusual

    Two signals are combined. Reconstruction error responds to structure the autoencoder never learned to express, and rises with brightness, size and noise. Local isolation, measured as mean cosine distance to the twenty nearest neighbours in the self-supervised embedding, responds to objects lacking close analogues, and rises along the sparse tail of common classes.     Limitations

    • Scale. These networks are small, trained at 65 pixels on a laptop CPU. Absolute accuracy sits well below the published state of the art on this dataset, where Pandya and collaborators report 95.5 percent from a larger D16-steerable network at higher resolution on four A100 GPUs. The claim here concerns the shape of the label-efficiency curve under a controlled comparison.
    • Model selection. The validation set is held at 10 percent of the data for every label fraction. A strictly realistic low-label protocol would shrink it in proportion. Holding it fixed flatters the low-fraction points in absolute terms, and does so identically for both architectures.
    • Seeds. Error bars are one standard deviation over three seeds, sufficient to separate the architectures and too few for a tight interval. The crossing fraction is reported as a per-seed range for this reason.
    • Group coverage. Only C8 was trained. Dihedral groups and other orders remain untested here, and the literature reports gains from both.
    • Label quality. Galaxy Zoo labels are aggregated human votes carrying real disagreement, concentrated between tight and loose spirals and among disturbed galaxies. That disagreement bounds every accuracy figure above.
    • Self-supervised budget. The contrastive encoder saw 39 epoch-equivalents, short by the standards of the method. The reported probe accuracy is a floor.
